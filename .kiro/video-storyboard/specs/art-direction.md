# Art direction: Dead Air -- 1873 storyboard

## Look
A quiet Victorian field notebook in the dark. These are working storyboard pages, not game screens. Each slide feels like a film-planning card lying beside a telegraph set at night: charcoal background, warm paper, and one candlelit detail. Nothing glows like a game menu. Nothing looks like a corporate deck.

## Colour
Use near-black charcoal #11100E for the background. Use old-paper #E8DDC5 for main text and card surfaces. Use aged brass #B78A45 for times, key lines, and small markers. Use muted ember red #9E493F only for TODO badges and warnings. Use faded grey #9B958A for secondary notes. Keep the palette restrained. Never use bright blue, neon, gradients, or large red areas.

## Type
Use a readable serif face for titles, such as Georgia, with normal sentence case. Use a simple sans-serif face for timings, notes, labels, and body text, such as Calibri. Titles feel printed; planning notes feel typed. Voice lines use quotation marks and are slightly larger than notes. Avoid all-caps except the game title and short labels.

## Decoration
Use fine brass rules, small telegraph-dash marks, and very faint paper grain. Use a thin hand-drawn border only on key visual slides: hook, Resurrection, close, and end card. Do not put a border on every slide. A small candle-flame mark can show the current video section. Never use stock art, emojis, fake UI, heavy shadows, or busy backgrounds.

## Storyboard cards
Each scene slide has a clear time stamp at top right, a short scene name at top left, a large footage frame in the middle, and the voice line below it. Footage frames use a warm-paper outline and a small caption such as "GAMEPLAY" or "SCREEN RECORDING". Slides with a real file or app screen use its screenshot as the main visual. Slides without footage use the user's own drawing or restrained line art.

## Planning slides
The title is spare: title, subtitle, one flame, and small prize labels. The timeline uses a single brass line with six quiet blocks, not a colourful chart. The rules slide is a pale-paper checklist on charcoal. The end card is almost black, with the prize words in warm paper and a small fading flame.

## TODO treatment
Every unfinished claim gets one small muted-ember badge: "TODO — ONLY IF TRUE". Place it beside the time stamp. Keep the badge visible but small. It is a recording reminder, not a design feature.

## Density and movement
Use one idea per slide. Give screenshots and footage frames room to breathe. Keep labels short. Let the visual weight sit in the footage, candle, or line art, not in paragraphs of text. Timeline and checklist slides may have more text, but keep their lines wide and easy to scan.
